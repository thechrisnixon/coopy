# Thai-Style Stir-Fried Ground Pork with Basil (Pad Bai Horapa or Pad Ka-Prao)

Serves 4 at 1× · Prep 15 minutes active · Total 15 minutes

Tags: Thai, stir-fry, wok, pork, quick, main

## Sources

From a single source.

**A — J. Kenji López-Alt, "The Wok: Recipes and Techniques" (via WBUR Here & Now)**
<https://www.wbur.org/hereandnow/2022/03/08/j-kenji-lopez-alt-wok-cookbook>

## Ingredients

*Quantities are 1× — one batch, exactly as the source wrote them.*

- **6** medium garlic cloves, peeled (about 2 tablespoons/16 g)
- **1** medium shallot (about 1½ ounces/45 g), peeled
- **2 tablespoons** peanut, rice bran, or other neutral oil (30 ml)
- **1 pound** lean ground pork, chicken, or beef or crumbled firm tofu (450 g)
  - *Hand-chopped pork sirloin gives the best texture; supermarket ground meat is easiest.*
- **⅓ pound** trimmed green beans, cut into ½-inch pieces (150 g)
  - *Not traditional, but a nice way to break up the meatiness.*
- **2 tablespoons** fish sauce (30 ml), plus more to taste
- **1 teaspoon** granulated sugar or palm sugar (4 g), more or less to taste
- **1 tablespoon** dark soy sauce (15 ml), optional
  - *Omit soy and oyster sauce (and season with more fish sauce) for a more traditional version.*
- **1 tablespoon** oyster sauce (15 ml), optional
  - *Omit for a more traditional fish-sauce-only seasoning.*
- **1½ ounces** fresh basil leaves (45 g; about 1½ cups packed)
  - *Holy basil (bai ka-prao) is most authentic; Thai purple or sweet Italian basil also work.*
- kosher salt, a small pinch (for the mortar)

### To Serve

- Steamed jasmine or short-grain rice
- Nam Pla Prik (optional)
- Extra-Crispy Fried Eggs (optional; page 114)

## Method

1. Cut the trimmed green beans into ½-inch pieces. If Using a Mortar and Pestle: Roughly chop the garlic and shallot and place in the mortar. Add a small pinch of kosher salt. Pound with the pestle until roughly mashed (no need to form a paste here). If Not Using a Mortar and Pestle: Mince the garlic and shallot by hand or in a minichopper.
2. Combine the oil and garlic and shallot mixture in a wok or skillet and place over medium-high heat. Cook, stirring frequently, until the aromatics are lightly softened and the oil is fragrant, about 30 seconds after it starts sizzling.
3. Add the meat or tofu and cook, stirring and tossing while breaking it up with a spatula until no longer pink, about 2 minutes.
4. Add the green beans and cook, stirring and tossing, until bright green and crisp-tender, about 1 to 2 minutes. Add the fish sauce, sugar, soy sauce (if using), and oyster sauce (if using). Continue to cook until most of the liquid is evaporated but the mixture is still nice and moist, about 1 minute.
5. Remove the wok or skillet from the heat. Add the basil and toss to combine until wilted. Serve immediately with rice, nam pla prik, and extra-crispy fried eggs.

## Notes

For best results, start with a whole piece of pork sirloin (or chicken breast, or firm tofu, or fish, or beef sirloin, or any lean meat really) and chop by hand. For easiest results, use ground meat from the supermarket.

Beans are not a traditional addition to the stir-fry, but they can be delicious and are a nice way to break up the meatiness.

If you want a more traditional version of the dish, omit the dark soy sauce and oyster sauce and season with more fish sauce to taste.

For the most authentic results, look for holy basil at an Asian supermarket (bai ka-prao). Thai purple basil or plain old sweet Italian basil can be used in its place.

A mortar and pestle will produce the most flavorful results, but you can easily make the dish without one. Excerpted from "The Wok" by J. Kenji López-Alt, copyright © 2022, republished with permission of W. W. Norton & Company.
